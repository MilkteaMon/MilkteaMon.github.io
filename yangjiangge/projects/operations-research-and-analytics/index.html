NUS · CDE2605 UROP

Signal tower placement with mixed-integer programming

A Gurobi mixed-integer model for signal tower placement, coverage and capacity, extended to land reclamation cost and pricing scenarios through CDE2605 UROP. Supervised by Dr Wang Guanyi at NUS, who holds a PhD from Georgia Tech.

Completed

Operations research

Download project report (PDF)

01 / Context

The problem

Meet communication demand at minimum tower installation and assumed reclamation cost, accounting for coverage, capacity and site eligibility in a simplified grid representation of Singapore.

02 / Purpose

The objective

Choose tower types and locations, then explore how assumed reclamation costs change feasible placement and modelled total cost.

03 / Progress

Current status

Completed

Completed CDE2605 UROP study with a mixed-integer formulation, Python/Gurobi implementation and numerical planning scenarios.

04 / Ownership

My contribution

Formulated and implemented the optimisation models and documented the scenarios in a CDE2605 final report under Dr Wang Guanyi at NUS (PhD, Georgia Institute of Technology).

05 / Method

The approach

  • Represent the planning region as 500 m by 500 m cells with simplified demand, signal contribution and eligible placement locations.
  • Compare Micro, Small and DAS tower types using different capacity, range and installation-cost assumptions.
  • Formulate integer tower counts and reclamation decisions with demand satisfaction, capacity and site constraints; implement the model in Python/Gurobi.
  • Use Excel-based demand and land matrices to inspect modelled tower placements and run reclamation-cost sensitivity scenarios.

06 / Evidence

What is available

  • The downloadable CDE2605 final report includes the mathematical formulation, grid assumptions, demand scenarios and Python/Gurobi code appendix.
  • The tower-placement output shown below is extracted from Figure 13 of that report. Results describe the academic model and its stated assumptions.

07 / Looking ahead

Limitations & next steps

  • Population is distributed uniformly within planning areas and signal propagation is simplified; the report also uses randomised demand scenarios.
  • Branch-and-price is discussed as future work and was not implemented in the reported study.
  • Modelled costs and placements are scenario results, not observed deployment outcomes or recommendations for an actual telecom network.
